Run order assumption: We run tickets in sequential order (101 → 102 → 103) starting from the $3,400.00 checking balance on shop date 2026-08-31.

Ticket 101

customer_order

Tauhid Zaman: 1x CC-TEE-WHITE size S, customer_order

Expected

awaiting_approval
Who Boss calls first & why
Boss reads the ticket with get_ticket and calls Inventory first because it is a customer order for physical merch (CC-TEE-WHITE size S) and we need to verify if we have stock on the shelf before doing anything else.
Handoff chain & who is left out
Boss → Inventory → Accounting → Customer Service, and Customer Service’s reply returns up the chain to Boss (not a new handoff, since 3 handoffs is our depth cap). Inventory sees 0 units of size S in Aisle B and sees linked Invoice 501 ($840.00 to Bulldog Print Co for a rush reprint), so Inventory hands off directly to Accounting (specialist-to-specialist) to handle the bill. Accounting verifies Invoice 501 was due 2026-08-28 (3 days overdue against desk.date_today 2026-08-31), checks cash ($3,400.00 available), stages the $840.00 payment, and hands off directly to Customer Service to draft the update for Tauhid: size S is out of stock, the reprint is waiting on payment approval, and the tee should be ready about 5 days after approval. Facilities is deliberately left out because this ticket has nothing to do with the shop lease.
Expected MCP tools
Boss uses get_ticket; Inventory uses check_order_fulfillment (plus read_table on inventory/vendors if needed); Accounting uses check_available_cash and read_table on invoices, then calls stage_payment for Invoice 501 ($840.00), which automatically moves the ticket to awaiting_approval; Customer Service only reads to confirm facts before drafting the reply.
Expected end state
awaiting_approval (since $840.00 is staged for Invoice 501 and a human has to click approve before cash moves and the ticket resolves).

rent_notice

Expected

awaiting_approval
Who Boss calls first & why
Boss reads the ticket with get_ticket and calls Facilities first because this is a property/lease rent notice for the Chapel Street shop (Lease 1), which falls strictly under Facilities’ scope.

Boss → Facilities → Accounting, and Accounting’s result returns up the chain to Boss. Expected end state
awaiting_approval (waiting for a human to approve the $2,400.00 rent payment, which will take uncommitted checking down from $2,560.00 to $160.00).

Ticket 103

price_override

Yale AI Club: 20x CC-HOOD-NAVY size M bulk discount, price_override

Expected

resolved
Who Boss calls first & why
Boss reads the ticket with get_ticket and calls Inventory first because before we can quote a bulk discount on 20 navy hoodies, we have to check how many size M hoodies are actually sitting in Aisle A and what the restock math looks like.
Handoff chain & who is left out
Boss → Inventory → Accounting → Customer Service, and Customer Service’s reply returns up the chain to Boss (not a new handoff, since 3 handoffs is our depth cap). Inventory checks stock and finds only 8 size M hoodies in Aisle A (short by 12 units; restocking 12 from Bulldog Print Co takes 5 lead days and costs 12 × $22.00 = $264.00). Inventory hands off directly to Accounting (specialist-to-specialist) to check our cash position and set the bulk override price. Accounting sees that after Ticket 101 ($840.00) and Ticket 102 ($2,400.00), uncommitted cash is down to $160.00, meaning we cannot stage the $264.00 vendor restock right now without going negative (and if we had run 103 before 102, spending $264.00 would have left $2,296.00 and caused us to miss the $2,400.00 rent!). Accounting sets a profitable bulk discount price per hoodie well above the $22.00 unit cost (down from the $58.00 list price), refuses to stage the $264.00 restock out of shop cash, and hands off directly to Customer Service to pitch the quote to Yale AI Club (deliver the 8 in-stock hoodies now at the discounted rate, and order the remaining 12 with a 5-day lead time once customer payment/deposit clears). Facilities is deliberately left out.
Expected MCP tools
Boss uses get_ticket; Inventory calls quote_bulk_order to get stock (8 on hand), unit cost ($22.00), list price ($58.00), and vendor lead days (5 days); Accounting uses check_available_cash ($160.00 remaining) and read_table on pricing, and calculates the discount without calling stage_payment; Boss records the quote and marks the ticket resolved with update_ticket (only Boss has it).
Expected end state
resolved directly (because no shop cash is staged or spent on this run).
